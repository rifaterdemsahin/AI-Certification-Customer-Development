# What AI Brings — and the Skills Gap It Creates (2020–2026 Timeline)

**Version:** 1.0.0
**Date:** 2026-08-01
**Purpose:** Document, year by year, what generative/agentic AI has actually delivered to the economy and the specific skills gaps it has opened up — as the evidentiary grounding for the TAM/SAM/SOM market-sizing "formula" published in `comp-market.html`, which previously cited figures (25M/3M/50k) with no source. This report replaces those unsourced assumptions with cited research and is now referenced directly from that page.

---

## 1. Year-by-Year Timeline

### 2020 — Foundation
- OpenAI released GPT-3 (175B parameters) via a private API beta in June 2020, the first commercially available large language model at that scale.
- Roughly 20% of global businesses had begun adopting AI in some form.
- **Skills gap opened:** almost none yet at the practitioner level — this was a research/early-adopter tool, not something the general workforce needed to be certified on.

### 2021 — Generative Expansion
- OpenAI launched DALL-E, combining language understanding with image generation.
- **Skills gap opened:** early "prompt design" know-how, confined to a small technical community; no formal training or certification market existed.

### 2022 — The Breakthrough
- ChatGPT launched November 30, 2022, becoming the fastest-growing consumer application in history — 100 million users within two months.
- Anthropic introduced Claude 1, emphasizing safety-first design.
- Stable Diffusion open-sourced high-quality image generation.
- **Skills gap opened:** general-purpose "how do I use this at work" literacy gap opens almost overnight, across every knowledge-work role simultaneously — the first time AI skill demand outran available training content.

### 2023 — Competitive Acceleration
- GPT-4 (March 2023) added multimodal capability; Anthropic's Claude became the primary competitor, positioned on safety and enterprise trust.
- McKinsey estimated generative AI could add $2.6–4.4 trillion in annual economic value across 63 use cases in 16 business functions, while noting the technology could automate 60–70% of employee time on existing tasks (versus ~50% for prior automation waves) — and, contrary to assumptions that automation hits low-skill work hardest, McKinsey found generative AI has a *larger* effect on higher-wage, highly educated knowledge workers.
- WEF's 2023 Future of Jobs Report had 44% of workers' core skills expected to change by 2030.
- **Skills gap opened:** the first formal "prompt engineering" job title appears; enterprises begin asking how to govern, secure, and audit LLM usage — a governance/architecture skill gap with no established curriculum yet.

### 2024 — Mainstream Business Integration
- 78% of organizations reported using AI in at least one business function (up sharply from prior years).
- OpenAI shipped GPT-4o; Anthropic shipped Claude 3.5 — both faster and cheaper to run, accelerating enterprise deployment beyond pilots.
- McKinsey projected at least 14% of employees globally would need career changes by 2030, with Europe alone needing up to 12 million occupational transitions — double the pre-pandemic pace.
- Cloud-certified professionals were already earning 20–25% more than non-certified peers, and roughly 70% of IT professionals were expected to hold a cloud-related certification by 2025 — establishing the pre-existing "certification pays" pattern that AI credentials are now extending.
- **Skills gap opened:** "AI integration" (RAG pipelines, tool use, agent orchestration, cost/latency management) becomes a distinct engineering discipline; most existing degree programs and bootcamps have no curriculum for it yet.

### 2025 — Advanced Reasoning & Agentic AI
- Reasoning models (o3), Claude Opus 4.5, and GPT-5 shipped; AI copilots became embedded across enterprise applications.
- Claude Code reached general availability and, by late 2025, was responsible for roughly 4% of all public GitHub commits, reaching a $1B annualized run rate within six months — a concrete signal that agentic coding tools were being adopted at production scale, not just experimented with.
- WEF's Future of Jobs Report 2025 (published January 2025) found: 63% of employers cite the skills gap as their primary barrier to business transformation; AI and big data skills saw a 17-percentage-point jump in projected importance versus 2023; 80% of employers planned to upskill staff on AI and two-thirds planned to hire specifically for AI skills; yet 75% of respondents said they had no access to formal AI education/training at their own company. The report projects 92 million jobs displaced and 170 million created by 2030 (net +78 million), with 39% of core skills expected to change by 2030.
- LinkedIn's Skills on the Rise 2025 report listed prompt engineering among the 15 fastest-growing skills; LinkedIn recorded a 20-fold increase in members adding AI skills to their profiles since 2016.
- 60% of organizations reported expecting a shortage of skilled cloud professionals in 2025, with cloud-computing skill demand itself projected to grow 25% in the year.
- **Skills gap opened:** "agentic system design" (multi-agent orchestration, guardrails, autonomous coding agents) — a skill that essentially did not exist as a job requirement 18 months earlier.

### 2026 — Agentic Era & Certification Formalization
- AI systems routinely complete 30+ minute autonomous development tasks; agentic coding tools (e.g., GPT-5.1 Codex-Max class models) push further into multi-step autonomy.
- LinkedIn's Skills on the Rise 2026 report names AI engineering, prompting, and model optimization as the fastest-growing skill category globally.
- **Anthropic formalized the skills gap into an actual credentialing market**: it launched the Claude Certification Program in 2026 (Claude Certified Architect – Foundations in March, then Associate/Developer/Architect-Professional exams in July), delivered via Pearson VUE, explicitly to validate the orchestration/latency/multi-agent/security skills the market had been demanding informally for two years. It simultaneously launched the Claude Partner Network's Services Track, which ties consulting-firm status tiers to certified-practitioner headcounts (10 for Select, 100 for Preferred, 1,000 for Global Premier) — turning the AI skills gap into a contractual, budgeted line item for large IT services firms (see `reports/market-validation-argument-v1.0.md` for the full argument).
- **Skills gap now formally recognized and priced:** what was informal, self-taught "AI literacy" in 2022–2024 is, by 2026, a proctored, scored, renewable, vendor-administered credential with enterprise partner-tier requirements attached to it.

## 2. What This Means for the Skills-Gap Formula

Read across the timeline, three distinct skills gaps opened in sequence, each with a different market size and urgency:

1. **General AI literacy (2022–2023):** broad, shallow, affects nearly all knowledge workers; largely addressed today by free content and internal enablement, not a paid-certification opportunity.
2. **AI integration engineering (2024–2025):** narrower, technical, addresses the "how do we actually ship this safely and cost-effectively" question; this is where cloud-style certification demand (per the 20–25% pay premium and 70% cloud-certification-adoption precedent) is now repeating for AI specifically.
3. **Agentic/architect-level AI skills (2025–2026):** the newest and narrowest gap, now the one Anthropic's own Certified Architect – Professional exam targets directly — and the one this business's flagship curriculum is built around.

The TAM/SAM/SOM figures published in `comp-market.html` (previously unsourced) should be read against gap #3 specifically, not the broader "everyone needs AI literacy" market — gap #3 is real, dated to 2025–2026, and has a named, priced credential behind it, but it is also the newest and least-tested of the three, with no multi-year adoption data yet (the certification itself is only months old as of this writing).

---

## 3. Recommended Update to the Published Formula

`comp-market.html` currently states TAM = 25M developers, SAM = 3M developers seeking cloud/ML/LLM certifications, SOM = 50,000 reachable within 3 years — with no citation. This report grounds that formula as follows (see the corresponding edit made to `comp-market.html`):

- TAM anchor: WEF's Future of Jobs Report 2025 finding that AI/big data is the fastest-rising in-demand skill category, with 80% of employers planning AI upskilling — supports a large, growing addressable population, though WEF does not itself publish a specific headcount matching "25M."
- SAM anchor: the pre-existing cloud-certification base rate (70% of IT professionals expected to hold a cloud credential by 2025; 20–25% pay premium for certified staff) as the closest available proxy for how many technical professionals convert general AI interest into paid certification behavior.
- SOM anchor: Anthropic's own Claude Partner Network practitioner-quota structure (10 / 100 / 1,000 per tier) as a bottom-up, named-vendor data point for realistic near-term certified-practitioner volume, rather than a top-down guess.

This does not fully validate the specific 25M/3M/50k numbers — no source ties to those exact figures — but it replaces "no citation at all" with named, dated, sourced anchors the numbers can be checked against and revised from.
