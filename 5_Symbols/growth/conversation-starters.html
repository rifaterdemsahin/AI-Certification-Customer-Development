🗣️ Conversation Starters for Events

A one-liner introduction is often the entire first impression at a meetup, conference, or networking event — before a business card, before a LinkedIn add, before anyone reads a single page of this site. This page is a swipe file of ready-to-use openers plus short role-play scripts for the follow-up exchange, so the founder (or anyone representing the business) has something practiced rather than improvised on the spot. Inside the Skool room, the same rule applies: ask unfinished questions instead of lecturing — see Golden Rules of Community Building.

💬 One-Line Openers

Each variant leads with a different angle — audience help, ambition, or personal coaching — so the right one can be picked for the room. All stay compliant with the site-wide no-guarantee rule: none of these promise a passed exam, only help, practice, or coaching.

🎓 The Helper Angle
"I help people get AI certifications." — simple, outcome-first, easy to say to a stranger in five seconds. Works best with non-technical audiences (recruiters, generalist founders) who just need to know the category.
🚀 The Ambition Angle
"We push the limits of AI and help get certifications." — frames the work as frontier/edge-of-capability, not just exam-prep admin. Works best with technical audiences (engineers, AI practitioners) who respond to a harder, more interesting framing.
🧑‍🏫 The Coach Angle
"I coach people to push AI to the limits." — makes it personal and 1:1, emphasizes the founder's own hands-on role rather than a faceless product. Works best when the founder is the one talking, leaning on his own credibility as someone who has already passed the exam himself.

➕ More Openers to Try

  • 🎬"I make animated videos that turn dry certification manuals into something people actually finish."
  • 🌙"Every Sunday night I run a free live study group for people prepping for AI certifications."
  • 🏅"I run a program that gets technical people exam-ready for Anthropic's Claude certification."
  • 🧩"Think Duolingo, but for passing your AI certification exam."
  • 👥"I built a community where people preparing for the same AI exam solve problems together live."
  • 📈"Companies are starting to require AI certifications — I help people get ahead of that."

🎭 Role-Play Scripts

Short two-line exchanges to rehearse. The goal of each is the same: end with either a follow-up question back to them, or an invite (the free Sunday cohort, or the newsletter) — never a hard pitch on the first exchange.

Scenario 1 — The Skeptical Engineer

Them: "Another cert-prep course? There's a hundred of those."
You: "Fair — most are slide decks. Mine's animated video plus a live Sunday group where we solve real problems together, not just watch. What cert are you working toward?"

Scenario 2 — The Curious Recruiter

Them: "What do you actually do?"
You: "I help people get AI certifications — animated videos plus live practice sessions. Companies are starting to gate consulting-partner status on how many certified staff they have, so it's becoming a real hiring signal."

Scenario 3 — The Fellow Founder

Them: "How's the business going?"
You: "Early — solo, second job, but the free Sunday cohort has run 8 weeks straight and people are already bringing friends. Validating before I go all-in."

Scenario 4 — The Non-Technical Contact

Them: "AI certification? What's that even mean?"
You: "It's like a professional exam, but for AI skills — proves someone can actually build and manage AI systems safely. I coach people through prepping for it."

1. Persona-Pitch Alignment

Grade: C+

Critique: The commenter is a 25-year platform veteran discussing enterprise deployment gates. Pitching "moving into new AI roles / get certified" frames the community as junior/reskilling rather than an elite Forward Deployed Engineering guild.

2. Link Placement & Algorithm

Grade: C

Critique: Dropping raw URLs in first-level comments can trigger LinkedIn's external link reach damping and feels like automated spam to third-party observers reading the comments.

3. Customer Discovery (CD)

Grade: B-

Opportunity: Marcel is an ideal advisor/co-creator (H29/H30). A prompt asking him about his pipeline gates turns a passive comment into an interview transcript or Sunday guest spot.

4. Speed & Responsiveness

Grade: A

Strength: Replying quickly preserves conversation velocity while the post is still active in the LinkedIn newsfeed algorithm.
