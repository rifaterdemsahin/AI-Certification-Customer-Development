🧠 Rigorous Sanity Check of the Business Hypothesis

Let us red-team the premise: “Spending $10,000 on high-grade content and recapturing $10,000 via community validates an autonomous small business better than selling courses through Coursera.”

✅ Point 1: Unit Economics & Margin Capture

Validated

Thesis: On Coursera, generating $10,000 of course sales yields only $2,000 (20%) to the author. In an owned Skool community, $10,000 in customer payments yields ~$9,500 after payment processing.

Sanity Verdict: 5x higher cash capture per transaction. 40 paid members at $250 equals $10k direct, whereas Coursera requires 200–500 platform subscribers to return $2k to the instructor.

✅ Point 2: High-Ticket Upsell & FDE Alliances

Validated

Thesis: Coursera is a static video library with a 3–5% completion rate. Skool combines curriculum with live Sunday sprints, 1-on-1 VIP presets menus (Presets Pivot), and recruiter promotion.

Sanity Verdict: Coursera cannot sell a £500–£1,000/day contractor transformation (H30). Community members buy career velocity and enterprise positioning, not video files.

⚠️ Point 3: Distribution Friction (The Risk)

Mitigated

The Trap: Coursera has 100M+ registered learners and enterprise B2B sales reps. An independent business must generate its own discovery traffic.

Mitigation: 100% free unredacted YouTube modules (Jab Jab Left Hook) rank on search intent for "Claude Certified Architect", funneled to Skool via founder-recorded CTAs (Voice SOP).

⚠️ Point 4: Founder Bandwidth & Second Job

Constrained

The Trap: The founder works a 40h/week enterprise contract role alongside running the community (~65h total weekly load, see Bandwidth Ledger).

Mitigation: Batch-answering comments in weekly cycles, living hub static posts to prevent spam (Garden Playbook), and promoting 10 True Regulars to community moderators.
