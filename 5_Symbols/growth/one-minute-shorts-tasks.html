👩‍🏫 The "Tech with Nana" Pedagogy (Feel Smart, Not Stupid)

1. Intuitive Everyday Analogies

Never introduce an abstract term without anchoring it in human experience first. (e.g. RAG is a briefing memo before a meeting; Fine-tuning is hiring a 5-year industry veteran).

2. Visual First-Principles Diagrams

Clean 2D animations and motion slides (Google Flow / Canva) with bold typography and contrasting nodes. No intimidating terminal screens or complex math formulas.

3. Sub-60s Actionable Clarity

5–6 scenes of 8–10 seconds each. Cut all filler and throat-clearing. End on a crisp takeaway: "Day N of the AI Library — Day N+1 is [Next Term]".

1. Machine Learning (ML)

💡 Nana Analogy: Instead of writing 10,000 rules to describe a dog, you show a child 10,000 photos of dogs and cats until their brain spots the patterns automatically.
60s Beat Sheet:
  1. Hook (0-8s): How do computers learn without being programmed rule-by-rule?
  2. Old Way (8-18s): Traditional coding: If has whiskers, if has fur → breaks on a fluffy cat.
  3. ML Way (18-32s): Feed 10,000 images into an algorithm; it finds the statistical patterns.
  4. Real-world (32-45s): Spam filters, Netflix recommendations, Tesla autopilot.
  5. Closer (45-55s): Day 1 done. Day 2 is Neural Networks.

Short 05 · Memory Queued

5. Context Window

💡 Nana Analogy: Your physical desk space. You can spread out 5 pages or 200 pages. Once the desk is full, older documents fall off the edge and are completely forgotten.
60s Beat Sheet:
  1. Hook (0-8s): Ever had ChatGPT forget what you said 10 minutes ago?
  2. The Desk (8-22s): Context window is short-term working RAM, not permanent hard drive memory.
  3. Evolution (22-35s): From 4k tokens (GPT-3) to 200k (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) to 2M (Gemini).
  4. Risk (35-48s): Paste a 500-page book → attention degradation and truncation.
  5. Closer (48-56s): Next: How do we search millions of docs without overflowing the window? (Vectors).
