🎯 Top 3 Hypotheses to Test Face-to-Face — September 2026

ASK IN YOUR OWN WORDS — NEVER READ THE HYPOTHESIS ALOUD

Per the Interview Guide's Listening Mandate: a leading "would you pay for X?" question gets a polite lie, not evidence. Steer the conversation toward the topic, then listen for the specific "kill" or "confirm" signal named under each hypothesis below.

#1H3 — Audience will pay for certification prep
Why it's #1: Foundational Stage 1 premise with no dependency — it is the root of the entire revenue model. Feeds H19 (Discovery-stage exit condition), and every downstream cohort/pricing hypothesis (H5, H8, H9) assumes this is true. Named contacts (Mehmet, Charles) have given the strongest explicit "I would not buy" signals on the whole site — this needs more real evidence, not less.
Current status (2026-09-10): 🟡 In Progress. Real signals so far: Charles reached out directly for exam-prep help; 5 discovery calls completed via Baris's WhatsApp channel; exam price elasticity observed (£75 Associate → £125 Developer). Countervailing: Mehmet ("I would definitely not buy it... I wouldn't take a course from you") and Charles ("I didn't pay them" re: competitors) — neither is a $10/mo or $250 signal either way. Newsletter's >5% signup target still unmeasured.

Questions to ask face-to-face:

  • "What's the last course, book, or prep tool you paid for out of your own pocket? How much, and why that one?"
  • "Did you sign up for any waitlist or newsletter for something exam-related recently? What made you do it?"
  • "You've watched a few of the free Sunday sessions / videos — what would it take for you to actually put a card down?"
Kills if: every answer is hypothetical ("I'd probably pay for...") rather than a real past purchase, and nobody can name a specific dollar amount they've spent on similar prep before.
#2H5 — Cohorts sell out organically without paid ads
Why it's #2: Foundational Stage 2 premise — feeds H9 explicitly (it is the literal revenue basis of the $10k Stage 2→3 gate), feeds H8 (peer-accountability evidence), and feeds H16 (the organic-CAC baseline any paid ad would be benchmarked against). It is the single biggest unanswered question standing between "~30 free Skool joins" and the first real $250–$500 paid seat.
Current status (2026-09-10): 🟡 In Testing. 8 Skool signups measured against the 20–40-per-launch target; ~30 total joins, 100+ Skool conversations, but a thin live Sunday room (Session 10). One named referral chain (Bihar → Sude). Zero confirmed $250/$500 Share Screen enrollments or $10/mo paying memberships yet — everything so far is a free-tier join, not the paid conclusion this hypothesis needs.

Questions to ask face-to-face:

  • "Have you ever joined a live study group or cohort before? How did you hear about it, and did you pay?"
  • "What would a live weekly session need to give you — besides passing the exam — to be worth showing up for every time?"
  • "If Share Screen were $250 for the year starting today, what would stop you from joining right now versus waiting?"
Kills if: nobody can describe a concrete reason to pay beyond "maybe later," and word-of-mouth referral stays a single named pair rather than a repeating pattern. Never imply the exam pass itself is promised (H8 Premise 3).
#3H27 — Missing blueprint-mapped practice exams is a critical value blocker
Why it's #3: Foundational Stage 2 product-value risk with no dependency — feeds H8 (PMF) and H21 (the $29 bundle SKU). This is the hypothesis with the most concrete, repeated real-world failure evidence already on file (a real exam fail at 644/1000, two independent postponements), which makes it the highest-confidence one to keep probing rather than treat as settled.
Current status (2026-09-10): 🟡 In Testing. Jamal failed Foundations at 644/1000 (below 720 pass bar). Charles and Baris both independently postponed their exams citing prep gaps. Charles (2026-09-09), even after passing, still hunts a second sponsored channel (Udemy) for practice papers. Question bank production has started (2 of a targeted 60 questions published in the Skool Classroom).

Questions to ask face-to-face:

  • "When you were closest to booking (or postponing) your exam, what specific resource were you missing or searching for?"
  • "Have you used any mock exams or question banks to prep? Which ones, and did they feel mapped to the real blueprint domains?"
  • "If you sat the exam again tomorrow, what's the one thing you'd want to drill on first?"
Kills if: candidates report confidently passing or scheduling without ever seeking out practice questions — i.e. the gap turns out not to be load-bearing for their decision to book or delay.
