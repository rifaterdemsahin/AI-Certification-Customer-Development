📊 MVP Validation Metrics

What we measure in this phase:

Before scaling up cohort structures, we must prove that our MVP engages users sufficiently. YouTube Analytics acts as our validation lab. We stay focused on one problem only: helping the customer pass their certification exam — everything the MVP measures exists to prove the video content is clear and high-quality enough to serve that single goal, not to confuse or overwhelm the customer.

  • ⏱️
    Average View Duration (AVD):

    Goal is >40%. This is a video-quality signal specifically — it tells us the content is clear and not confusing the customer, not a proxy for revenue readiness or full-time viability (those are tracked separately). If views drop off within the first 30 seconds, we must refine our scripting style or video pacing.

  • 💬
    Organic Comment Sentiment:

    We track how many people ask for certification worksheets, code files, or additional study resources.

  • 🖱️
    Click-Through-Rate (CTR) on External Links:

    We track conversions from the video description (links to newsletters/practice exams) to establish funnel intent.

Reading this data point: the 68.5% average percentage viewed clears the >40% MVP watch gate defined above — the video-quality signal is real, not just hypothesized. YouTube's own "typical performance" comparison shows this video is currently running below the channel's usual reach (40 views vs. ~210 typical, 0.1 vs. ~5 watch-time hours), and subscriber change from the video is not yet measurable. In other words: small audience so far, but the people who do watch, watch to the end — a retention win, not yet a reach or subscriber-growth win (that's H13's bar). One video is a data point, not the catalog-wide average H10 requires.
