🎯 Make It About Them, Not About You

The single most lethal failure mode for veteran, high-earning technical founders ($1.3M+ enterprise contractor track record) is Me-Centricity (The Founder Ego Trap): building products that showcase personal brilliance, delivering monologues that demonstrate mastery, and treating audiences as spectators rather than equipping them as the heroes of their own career transformation. In customer development, if the spotlight is on you, the business dies. See H29 and H24.

📌 Executive Verdict & Core Law

“Nobody buys your expertise to admire you. They buy your scaffold so they can win.”

When a founder says “Look what I built”, customers feel alienated and small. When a founder says “Here is why you are stuck at £400/day, and here is how you cross £800/day as a Forward Deployed Engineer”, customers lean in, engage, and invest. Every historical failure in Rifat's portfolio traced back to centering the conversation on founder authority rather than the customer's urgent, bleeding problem.

💀 Post-Mortem of Previous Fails: The 5 Archetypes of “About You”

To avoid repeating past failures, we must deconstruct how “About You” stealthily corrupts every stage of product, delivery, marketing, and community architecture:

Fail Mode #1 Curse of Knowledge

The Intellectual Indulgence Trap

What happened: The founder built deeply sophisticated architectures, intricate multi-model pipelines, or esoteric theory because the founder found it intellectually stimulating.

Why it failed: The customer was drowning in fundamental hurdles (setting up API keys, parsing Claude exam blueprints, getting their CV noticed by recruiters). Complex abstractions overwhelmed them, leading to paralysis and dropouts.

Fail Mode #2 Lecture Trap

The Two-Hour Monologue

What happened: Live cohort sessions became 120-minute presentations where the founder spoke non-stop, advancing slide after slide, broadcasting answers to questions nobody asked.

Why it failed: Passive consumers do not retain information and do not feel ownership. The moment attendees became spectators instead of active problem-solvers, energy collapsed. (Pivoted on 2026-08-17 to the Party / Dialogue format).

Fail Mode #3 Story Inversion

The “Hero” vs. “Guide” Inversion

What happened: Positioning the founder as the heroic genius who has conquered $1.3M in contracts and knows everything, making the student feel perpetually junior and dependent.

Why it failed: In classic StoryBrand theory, the customer must always be Luke Skywalker; the founder must only be Yoda. When the founder competes with the customer for hero status, the customer leaves.

Fail Mode #4 Ego-Driven Hooks

“Look What I Made” Broadcasting

What happened: Content headlines and community posts framed around founder progress: “I spent 14 hours building this agent” or “Here is my new course outline.”

Why it failed: Zero viral reach or engagement. Audience attention is purely selfish: “What does this do for my salary, my resume, my weekend, and my exam?”

🏋️ The Hormozi Value Equation Inversion

Applying Alex Hormozi's Grand Slam Offer formula reveals why “About You” destroys business value:

$$\text{Perceived Value} = \frac{\text{Dream Outcome} \times \text{Perceived Likelihood of Achievement}}{\text{Time Delay} \times \text{Effort \& Sacrifice}}$$

❌ How “About You” Destroys the Fraction

  • 📉Decreases Perceived Likelihood: When the founder looks like a superhero, students think: “Rifat can do this because he is a genius contractor, but I could never replicate that.”
  • 📈Inflates Effort & Sacrifice: Complex explanations make passing the exam or landing contracts feel exhausting and unattainable.

✅ How “About Them” Maximizes the Fraction

  • 🚀Maximizes Dream Outcome: Anchors on their identity shift (from unverified coder to £800/day certified Forward Deployed Engineer).
  • ⚡Minimizes Time Delay: 1-page blueprints, flipped classroom micro-lessons, and instant CV recruiter referrals via Delivery Pilot.

🔍 The 5-Second Founder Pre-Flight Ritual (Checklist)

Before recording a video, hitting “Publish” on Skool, opening a Sunday live Zoom, or writing copy, execute this 6-point self-diagnostic:

1. The Pronoun Audit
Count the “I / Me / My” words vs. “You / Your / Our”. If “I” dominates, rewrite from the reader's perspective before publishing.
2. Whose Problem Is This?
Did a named student (Cheuk, Charles, Apo, Sude, Brian) explicitly ask for this, or did I invent it because it felt cool to build?
3. The 5-Minute Timer
In live calls, set a hard timer: 5 minutes of founder context, then hand screen control to a participant. No exceptions.
4. Public Credit Rule
Always cite the student who inspired the change: “Sude asked how to structure Claude memory, so here is the solution.”
5. The Hero Check
Does this asset make the student feel capable and empowered (Luke Skywalker), or does it make the founder look untouchable?
6. Concrete Financial / Exam Link
Does this directly help them pass the Pearson VUE exam or boost their contract day rate by £200+? If neither, cut it.
