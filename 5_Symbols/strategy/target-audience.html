Target Audience & Buyer Journeys

Detailed personas for the three market segments introduced in Market & Audience. This page maps who they are and how they move from awareness to purchase; see that page for TAM/SAM/SOM sizing (not duplicated here).

🚀 Persona 1: Dani, the Career Pivoter

  • Role:Mid-level backend engineer, 5 yrs experience
  • Goal:Reposition as an "AI architect" before boilerplate roles shrink
  • Pain Point:Job postings now list LLM/agent skills she doesn't have proof of
  • Buying Trigger:Sees a peer share a Claude Certified Architect badge on LinkedIn
  • Objection:"Will a badge actually matter to hiring managers?"

Journey

  1. Awareness: Finds an animated YouTube video ranking for "Claude AI Architect exam prep."
  2. Interest: Watches the structured Course tab, downloads a free exam blueprint cheatsheet.
  3. Consideration: Joins the newsletter to see cohort dates and pricing.
  4. Purchase: Enrolls in a $250–$500 cohort once a launch date is announced.
  5. Advocacy: Shares her LinkedIn certification badge, referring the next cohort's students.

👔 Persona 2: Marcus, the Tech Consultant

  • Role:Independent LLM/cloud infrastructure consultant
  • Goal:Win enterprise clients who now require named-certified consultants
  • Pain Point:Loses RFPs to firms that can list certified practitioner headcount
  • Buying Trigger:A client explicitly asks for Claude Partner Network practitioner credentials
  • Objection:Time cost — can he study around billable client hours?

Journey

  1. Awareness: Searches directly for exam prep after a client request.
  2. Interest: Skims the YouTube course for time-to-competency signals.
  3. Consideration: Compares the $10/mo membership vs. self-study; values live 1-1 support.
  4. Purchase: Enrolls directly in a cohort, skipping the membership tier — time matters more than price to him.
  5. Advocacy: Recommends the cohort inside his firm, a lead into the B2B channel (see H12 in Hypothesis Tracker).

🎓 Persona 3: Priya, the Top-Tier Graduate

  • Role:Final-year computer science student
  • Goal:Skip entry-level ranks by showing advanced credentials before graduating
  • Pain Point:Limited budget; can't justify a 40-hour paid course without proof it works
  • Buying Trigger:Free YouTube content proves the teaching style works before she pays anything
  • Objection:Price sensitivity — will look for student discounts or the cheaper membership tier

Journey

  1. Awareness: Discovers the channel via a recommended video, not a search.
  2. Interest: Binges several free lessons; comments with questions.
  3. Consideration: Joins the $10/mo membership first — the lowest-friction paid step.
  4. Purchase: Upgrades to a cohort once she has confidence and, often, a stipend or graduation gift budget.
  5. Advocacy: Shares in university/CS-club communities, a low-cost organic acquisition loop.

🧪 Real Behavior Pattern: The Mock-Exam Confidence Loop

Unlike the three illustrative personas above, this is a directly observed pattern from real discovery calls — not a composite. Charles, one of the named discovery-call contacts already tracked in H3 and H8, is a concrete example of it.

📅 The 3-week booking window
Exam takers typically book their real Pearson VUE exam slot about 3 weeks out, then cram study around that fixed deadline. This is a hard timing constraint, not a soft "someday" intent — the product has to be ready to consume immediately, not require weeks of onboarding.
📦 Packaged, easy to take
With only ~3 weeks of runway, candidates want something they can pick up and use immediately — not assemble from scattered docs. This is the direct product rationale behind the $29 Exam Prep Bundle's packaging.
😌 Passing the mock exam = relaxed
The mock exam isn't just a study tool — it's the specific moment that converts anxiety into confidence. Once a candidate passes the mock exam, they report feeling relaxed and exam-ready, which is the emotional "Gain" this product is actually selling (see Value Proposition).
