📝 The Constructive Notes

Eight notes a partner would put on the table. Severity is the partner's, not the founder's self-score.

VF1 · Do not raise venture capital at this stage

Blocking

“Come back when you are default-alive on the paid offer, not when the documentation is complete.”

Why a partner says this
Paul Graham's first question to an operating startup is whether it is default alive or default dead — can it reach profitability on the current trajectory without another raise? If the company is default dead, the conversation is about survival, not ambition. Venture capital is a power-law instrument: funds need a small number of outcomes large enough to return the fund. Writing a check into a pre-revenue, second-job, single-founder education project consumes partner time without a path to that return. Source: Graham, Default Alive or Default Dead (Oct 2015).
How it maps here
$0 revenue, no paid launch, founder on a contract role, comments answered in weekly batches. The business is default-alive only because it spends almost nothing — not because the offer has proven it can cover the founder's time. See Unit Economics (£525/day contracting benchmark vs. $15.63/hr per $250 seat) and Single-Founder Bandwidth.

What would close it: two consecutive paid cohort launches combining to $10,000 (H9). That is still not a raise trigger — it is the Stage 2 → 3 gate. A partner conversation starts after that, not before.

VF2 · The current model is not venture-scale

Thesis

“$10/month plus $250–$500 cohorts is a good lifestyle or SMB-education business. It is not a fund-returner unless the company becomes a talent marketplace.”

Why a partner says this
Bill Gurley's point in All Revenue is Not Created Equal (May 2011): recurring, expanding, high-switching-cost revenue earns a multiple; one-off, instructor-tied, low-switching-cost revenue does not. Consumer and prosumer education historically fails the 10× revenue club — high churn, platform CAC, no lock-in. A venture fund cannot underwrite a model whose ceiling is the founder's Sunday evening.
How it maps here
The live offer is three SKUs: $10/mo, $29 one-time bundle (H21), $250–$500 live cohort. The only stated path to a different multiple is H22 — placing certified graduates as Forward Deployed Engineers. That is explicitly out of scope for Discovery, Validation, and Creation. Treating H22 as the raise story today would be selling a later-stage company the current one has not earned the right to be.

What would close it: a working community at Stage 3 scale (H13), then a paid placement pilot with a named enterprise (H22). Until then, describe the business as what it is: cert-prep plus community.

VF3 · Free-cohort attendance is discovery, not traction

Evidence

“Eight Sundays with ~4 people and a WhatsApp thread is the right Customer Development work. It is not Product-Market Fit.”

Why a partner says this
Marc Andreessen's definition: Product-Market Fit is when the market is pulling the product out of the company — customers seek it, pay for it, and tell others without being sold. Until then, everything else is a rounding error. Source: Andreessen, The Only Thing That Matters (2007). Steve Blank's same distinction: getting out of the building is Stage 1; repeatable sales is Stage 2.
How it maps here
H5 is 🟡 In Testing — 8 Skool community signups, still no paid enrollment. H4's ~1% view→paid figure is unmeasured. Charles Andrews passed the exam using official Anthropic Skilljar material, not this product; that is meetup-channel evidence, not product evidence (see Candidate Exam Performance). First organic referrals (Brian, Sude) are real and small.

What would close it: paid seats at the $250–$500 tier (H5), then a second independent launch (H9). NPS >50 and a measured pass rate are H8 internal quality metrics, not a raise story.

VF4 · Top-down TAM will get discounted

Market

“25 million developers is a slide, not a market. Tell me who pays, why they have to, and how you reach the next 50 of them.”

Why a partner says this
Sequoia's Writing a Business Plan guidance treats market size as a bottom-up exercise: number of reachable buyers × willingness to pay, not a WEF headline. Partners have seen enough “TAM theater” that an unsourced 25M/3M/50k funnel is a negative signal unless the SOM layer is built from named accounts and observed conversion.
How it maps here
H6 is ⚠️ Claimed, unverified. Independent check in tam-sam-som-verification-v1.0 verified only the SAM layer (FDE hiring, 729% YoY). TAM and the ~50k SOM remain internal projections. The stronger market fact is narrower: Anthropic Partner Network tiers require 10 / 100 / 1,000 certified practitioners (H12).

What would close it: a bottom-up SOM from (a) paid individual conversions and (b) named Partner-Network firms with a stated seat count. Drop 25M from the first slide.

VF5 · Single-founder second-job is a ceiling, not a story

Team

“Founder-market fit is real. Bandwidth is the thing that kills you before a competitor does.”

Why a partner says this
CB Insights' recurring post-mortem, The Top Reasons Startups Fail, lists “not the right team” and “ran out of cash / got outcompeted” near the top. A solo founder on a contract role has no redundancy, no weekday delivery, and a hard weekly ledger. Ben Horowitz's caution in The Hard Thing About Hard Things is the inverse mistake: do not hire to look like a company before the offer works. Both can be true at once.
How it maps here
The site already admits this on Single-Founder Bandwidth and Risk Analysis (knowingly accepted). Hiring is gated to $100,000 ARR (H11); full-time transition is gated to the same number (H15). That is the correct sequence. It also means a partner cannot underwrite scale until that trigger is real.

What would close it: do not hire early. Close H9 first. Use H17 (Marianna / Cambridge onsite) as a second pair of hands for a single pilot, not as a team slide.

VF6 · Thin moat, plus platform and vendor dependency

Defensibility

“If YouTube changes the algorithm, or Anthropic changes the exam — or gates it to Partner firms — what is left?”

Why a partner says this
Peter Thiel's monopoly test in Zero to One (2014): a business that can be copied next quarter is not a venture business. NFX's Network Effects Manual distinguishes true network effects from a content library on a rented platform. YouTube reach and a vendor's certification program are both rented. Low switching cost is the default in test-prep.
How it maps here
Moat already lists the thin spots: no proprietary tech, single founder, YouTube dependency, Anthropic-curriculum dependency. Acidity Finding F12 is still open: Charles's prep came via an employer-sponsored Partner Network course — individual, non-Partner registration is unverified. If the exam is not openly bookable, the D2C funnel assumes an access path that may not exist. See Risk Register.

What would close it: confirm individual Pearson VUE registration (F12); grow an owned newsletter list; ship multi-vendor content (H14 — Nvidia, Microsoft, Google) so one vendor cannot zero the catalog.

VF7 · The product is incomplete without practice exams

Product

“Anxious buyers in week three do not buy animation. They buy retrieval practice mapped to the blueprint.”

Why a partner says this
In test-prep, the category table-stakes asset is a question bank. Clayton Christensen's jobs-to-be-done framing (used on Market Reality Check) says the job in the last three weeks before a Pearson VUE date is “tell me I am ready,” not “explain the concept again.” A catalog of explainers without mocks loses that job to whoever ships the bank first.
How it maps here
H27 is 🟡 In Testing: 2 of a targeted 60 blueprint-mapped questions published in Skool. The $29 bundle (H21) is planned, not sold. Discovery interviews (Charles, others) show candidates book ~2–3 weeks out and relax only after a mock. That is the conversion moment the current offer is thinnest on.

What would close it: finish the 60-question bank, sell the $29 SKU, and log whether mocks convert waitlist → paid. See Practice Exam Gap.

VF8 · B2B partner-quota demand is the real venture path

Opportunity

“Stop leading with YouTube. Lead with the firms that must buy certified headcount to keep their Anthropic tier.”

Why a partner says this
A budget that exists because a partner program gates revenue on certified headcount (10 / 100 / 1,000) is structurally better than a consumer who might spend $10. That is Gurley's “must-have vs. nice-to-have” revenue quality, applied to a channel. It is also how AWS training businesses got paid: the partner network created the quota; trainers filled it.
How it maps here
H12 is ⚪ Planned, but Premise 2 is no longer hypothetical: Charles Andrews, Senior Consultant at Capgemini Invent, publicly posted that Capgemini sponsored his Anthropic Partner Network certificate. That is a real employer-pay mechanic. It is not yet this business's revenue. Claude Partner Strategy maps the same mechanic to Blank's stages.

What would close it: ask 3–5 Registered-tier Partner firms whether they will buy bulk seats to hit a practitioner quota (the experiment already written in H12). One paid bulk order changes the conversation more than another animated video.

💰 Recommended Instrument

Constructive feedback includes saying which capital, if any, matches the stage. This is not a term sheet.

Now — no raise
Stay default-alive. Spend founder nights, not a fund's capital, to clear H9. Organic YouTube + LinkedIn + meetups only. Paid ads stay gated by H16.
After $10k × 2 — maybe angel
If H5 and H12 both have paid data, a small angel or pre-seed check can buy production capacity. Still not a typical seed: the model is education-plus-community until H22 is real.
After an H22 pilot — then VC
A certified-talent placement / recruiting company built on a proven community is the first story that can justify a venture multiple. Do not pitch that story before the community exists. See Company Building.
