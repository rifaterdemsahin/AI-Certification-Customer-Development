🧭 The Three Rules at a Glance

Good communities feel like a party. Bad ones feel like a lecture. The difference is not content quality. It is whether members have a reason to speak.

Rule 1

Don't lecture people

Thought-leader posts close the room. Unfinished questions open it. The vase is more useful half-made.

Rule 2

Build friendships

Strong rooms are made of strong relationships. Do the things that do not scale, on a second-job budget.

Rule 3

Curate the culture

A good community feels like a cozy village: inside jokes, model citizens, public praise, quiet moderation.

1. Don't Lecture People

The failure mode is already live on the Delivery Pilot board: founder-authored value posts with near-zero non-founder replies (posting sanity check). That is a lecture hall. A party asks people to bring something.

The problem

  • 📣Thought-leader articles and polished “value posts” train members to consume, not contribute.
  • 🎓A Classroom recording can lecture. The board cannot. The board is the party.
  • 🪞If every post is a finished vase, nobody has a reason to pick up the glaze.

The replacement

  • ❓Ask questions members can actually answer. Invite them into a decision, not a monologue.
  • 🌲Evergreen Hub Posts (Anti-Spam Guardrail): Do not flood members with separate posts for each topic. Maintain a single living post (e.g. "Courses") and nest details (like Claude Architect) inside, updating and nurturing that hub in place.
  • 👂Reply in comments more than you post. Listen more than you talk.
  • 🛠️Ask for help solving a real problem. Build in public. Bring people on the journey.

The vase, applied here

Skool's teaching example: do not post a finished glazed vase. Post the unglazed pot and ask which color. The unfinished object is the invitation. Delivery Pilot translations — all of these are questions Marianna, Sude, or Bayo can answer without being experts.

Lecture (don't) Party (do)
“Here is my 2,000-word write-up on RAG chunking.” “Sude, you hit PARA friction last week — what would you try first: smaller chunks, or a daily confusion note?”
“I published the official glossary. Read it.” “Which term from Sunday still feels fake? I'll pin the winner.”
“New Classroom module is live. Complete it.” “I'm picking next Sunday's hands-on. Vote: local MCP, or Qdrant from scratch?”
“Thoughts on the future of AI certifications?” “If you had 2 weeks like Charles, what would you cut from our prep list?”
“Lecture mode: 2-hour single-topic monologue on architecture.” Party mode (Live 2026-08-17 Validation): “Covering multiple topics, asking what people are going through, and making attendees talk. Result: accelerated learning pace & broadened perspective.”

Vague openers (“thoughts?”) are still lectures in disguise. Name a person, name a decision, leave something unfinished. For in-person openers see Conversation Starters.

2. Build Friendships

Good communities have strong relationships, not just strong content. The founder is a second-job operator, so the unscalable work is scoped to the named seats, not to a crowd. Sunday 9pm already is the weekly coffee hour. Do not add another live call.

Things that don't scale (this week)

  • 👋Daily hello + connect + notifications. Open members, greet anyone new by name, send a connect note, then clear the notification bell. Logged on the Task List daily pass.
  • 💬DM the founding three. Check in by name: “How's Skool going for you?” “What did you think of Sunday?”
  • 🔗Connect members to each other. Sude's vault work belongs next to anyone stuck on PARA. Introduce them in a thread, not just in your head.
  • 📞One 1:1 this week, not ten. A short call with whoever replies first. Bandwidth rule: one new 1:1 per week until the 10-regulars gate.
  • 🤝Meet in person when it is already happening. Triton Square / Venture Coffee / Cambridge. Do not invent a meetup. See Watering Holes.

Move private wins into the room

  • 📤If someone DMs something cool (a working vault, a passed quiz, a setup screenshot), ask them to post it in the community. Private praise does not build culture.
  • 🔁WhatsApp already has peer chatter (H8 Premise 2). Dual-post the same check-in on Skool for 2–4 weeks so the relationship has a public home.
  • 🌱A friendship that stays in WhatsApp never becomes a Skool regular. The true-regular definition is un-poked posts or Sunday attendance, not a private chat.
  • 📣Member-to-member intros are the live mechanism behind H20 (MAOT / referral). You cannot measure word-of-mouth if members never meet each other.
  • 🛡️Proven Retention Patterns: Showing up daily & caring (Nick Saraev), lower-tier downsell options (Skate IQ), and deleting bloated content (Al Jack) prevent member churn. See Reducing Churn & Retention.

3. Curate the Culture

Good communities feel like cozy villages. They have culture, inside jokes, elders, and a visible standard. The founder sets that standard in public, then lets model citizens carry it.

Make stars, not students

Publicly praise the first non-founder post. Name the person. Quote the useful bit. Build a deeper relationship with whoever shows up first — that person becomes the OG the next ten copy. Sude posting a vault screenshot is more valuable than another founder glossary.

Carrot and stick

Praise good behavior in public. Moderate bad behavior (spam, exam-dump requests, guarantee talk) in private, then remove it. The no-guarantee rule is culture, not legal copy — see Risk Analysis. Do not let the board become a dump of leaked questions.

Shared experiences

Sunday live is the shared ritual. Add one lightweight theme, not a second event: a weekly install challenge, a “confusion of the week,” a named award for the first person who ships a working Second Brain. Jokes, stories, and status beat another announcement.

Culture kit for a room of three

Do not wait for merch or a hundred members. A village of three still needs artifacts.

Inside joke
One recurring phrase from Sunday (name it, reuse it)
OG / elder
First non-founder poster becomes the named regular
Award
Public shout-out, not a badge farm. Status > points.
Theme
One week, one question. Then stop.
