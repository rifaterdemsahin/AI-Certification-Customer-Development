🛡️ Member Bandwidth Guardrails: Preventing Content Overload

Members don't cancel because there is too little content; they cancel when they feel overwhelmed and fall behind (Al Jack principle). Our audience consists of full-time software engineers, cloud architects, and tech leads balancing demanding jobs. This specification establishes concrete guardrails on weekly student time commitments to maintain high completion rates and prevent churn.

⏱️ How Much Time Does the Audience Have to Keep Up?

🎯 3 to 4 Hours / Week

The Sweet Spot

Realistic weekly allocation for working professionals. Provides meaningful exam progression without guilt, burnout, or falling behind.

Result: 72%+ completion rate & <5% monthly churn.
⚠️ 4 to 5 Hours / Week

The Caution Zone

Acceptable only during dedicated sprint weeks (e.g. 1 week before a scheduled Pearson VUE proctored exam).

Result: Fatigue starts accumulating after 2 consecutive weeks.
🚨 > 5 Hours / Week

The Overload Zone

Members miss one weekend session, feel hopelessly behind, stop checking the board, and silently cancel their subscription to avoid guilt.

Result: 18% completion rate & >20% cancellation churn.

🛑 5 Mandatory Founder Guardrails on Content Creation

Rule 1: "Delete Before Adding" (Al Jack Law)

Before adding a new 1-hour workshop, archive or consolidate an outdated module. The goal of the Classroom tab is minimum effective dose to pass the exam, not a library of infinite recordings.

Rule 2: Bite-Sized Micro-Modules (≤ 15 min)

Never publish an unedited 3-hour monolith in the Classroom without timestamps. Break lessons into 5 to 15 minute standalone micro-topics that a student can complete over a lunch break.

Rule 3: One Clear Call-to-Action Per Week

Do not bombard members with 4 different assignments. Give exactly ONE primary WIG lead action per week (e.g. "Complete Practice Quiz #3 by Friday 6pm").

Rule 4: Synchronized Founder & Student Bandwidth

The founder has 4–6 free hours/week (Founder Bandwidth). The student has 3–4 hours/week. A lean community protects both founder sanity and student retention.

Rule 5: Evergreen Hub Posts (Anti-Spam Protocol)

Do not spam the Skool feed with fragmented one-off posts (e.g. separate announcements for "Claude Course", "Nvidia Course", etc.). Instead, maintain single evergreen hub posts (e.g. "Courses") and nest vendor curriculum inside, updating and nurturing that living resource in place so members aren't inundated with notification clutter.
