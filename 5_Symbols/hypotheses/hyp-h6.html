📖 Long-Form: TAM, SAM, SOM

The three-line summary above compresses a lot of reasoning. The long-form version below walks through what each layer actually claims, what it's grounded against, and exactly how confident the site's own independent Perplexity verification (2026-08-05) says we should be in it — see Component: Market & Audience for the diagram and sourcing this expands on.

🌍 TAM — Total Addressable Market: ~25 million developers

TAM is the widest, least-filtered layer: everyone in the world who could ever conceivably become a customer if distribution, awareness, and channel reach were not constraints at all. For this business, that's every software developer worldwide who is now under pressure to adapt to AI-driven engineering — not just people who have heard of Claude, Anthropic, or this site, but the entire global population of developers facing the same structural shift H1 describes. The working figure is ~25 million developers, drawn loosely from global professional-developer population estimates (Evans Data puts the number in the ~26–28M range) and anchored directionally by the WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025: AI/big data is now the fastest-rising in-demand skill category, up 17 percentage points since 2023; 80% of employers say they plan to upskill staff in AI; 63% name the AI skills gap their single biggest transformation barrier; and 75% report they have no formal in-house AI training program to close it themselves. That last point matters for TAM specifically — it's evidence that the pressure to adapt is close to universal across the developer population, not confined to a niche already self-training on AI. Per the 2026-08-05 independent verification pass, TAM is explicitly ⚠️ unverified / a directional proxy: no single study states "25 million" as a number, so this is a working assumption that captures the right order of magnitude and the right direction (rising pressure, not falling), not an audited census figure. Treat it as the outer boundary of the addressable population, useful for sanity-checking that SAM and SOM aren't accidentally sized larger than the market that could ever contain them.

🎯 SAM — Serviceable Addressable Market: ~3 million developers

SAM narrows TAM down to the slice of developers who aren't just under abstract pressure to adapt, but are actually taking a certification-seeking action — specifically, developers actively pursuing cloud, machine learning, or LLM-specific (Anthropic/OpenAI-class) certifications. The working figure is ~3 million developers, grounded in two proxies documented in `comp-market.html`'s Formula Grounding section: roughly 70% of IT professionals are expected to hold a cloud-related certification by 2025, and certified staff already earn a 20–25% pay premium over uncertified peers — both signs that certification-seeking is a large, established, and financially rewarded behavior within the developer population, not a fringe activity. What sets SAM apart from TAM and SOM in this hypothesis is that it is the only layer the 2026-08-05 independent verification pass rates as ✅ empirically verified, via a completely independent, real-time signal: the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) role — an on-the-ground, client-embedded engineer who configures and operationalizes LLM/agent deployments — is a concrete, named embodiment of this exact SAM segment, and its hiring data is exploding. US FDE job postings grew 729% year-over-year (643 in April 2025 → 5,330 in April 2026, per Indeed), Christian & Timbers projects enterprise FDE demand to surge ~2,100% by end of 2026, and the share of companies planning to hire FDEs jumped from roughly 5–10% to 70% within two quarters. OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Cloud, Salesforce, and AWS are all named as fast-growing hirers, with mid-to-senior compensation ranging from a $170k base to $385k–$600k+ in total comp. Anthropic's own Claude Partner Network reinforces the same picture from the vendor side: it gates consulting-firm partner tiers directly on certified-practitioner headcount (10 for Select, 100 for Preferred, 1,000 for Global Premier) — the same mechanic AWS's Partner Network already uses. None of this independently confirms the exact "3 million" figure, but it is strong, current-year, sourced proof that the underlying enterprise behavior this SAM claim depends on — organizations actively funding people to deploy AI/LLM systems — is real and accelerating, not a hopeful guess.

🟣 SOM — Serviceable Obtainable Market: ~50,000 developers

SOM is the narrowest and most founder-specific layer: not "who could buy" (TAM) or "who is already certification-shopping" (SAM), but who this specific business, running through its specific channels, can actually reach within a specific time horizon. The working figure is ~50,000 developers reachable within 3 years, via the combination of Rifat's animated YouTube certification courses, cohort launches, and the newsletter funnel described elsewhere in this tracker (H3's newsletter capture, H4's YouTube→membership funnel, H7's click/conversion funnel, H13's 1,000x subscriber-growth target of ~30 → ~30,000). The only external anchor available for this layer is bottom-up rather than top-down: Anthropic's own Claude Partner Network practitioner-quota tiers (10 / 100 / 1,000 certified practitioners per tier) give a sense of what "obtainable" volumes already look like at the vendor-partner level, which is the same order of magnitude this SOM figure sits in once projected across a 3-year, multi-cohort, multi-video horizon. Per the 2026-08-05 independent verification pass, SOM is explicitly ⚠️ unverified / an internal projection — and structurally, it's the one layer that can never be externally verified in the way SAM was, because it isn't a market-wide behavior pattern, it's a claim about this one founder's execution capacity. It depends entirely on things tracked elsewhere in this tracker actually happening: the video production cadence holding at ~3/week (H4), the organic referral loop compounding beyond direct outreach (H5 Premise 6, first seen 2026-08-05 with Brian and Sude as early examples), the funnel's click/conversion rates holding up at scale (H7), and the subscriber-growth curve behind H13 actually compounding toward 30,000. Until those produce real multi-cohort data, ~50,000 should be read as an aspirational order-of-magnitude target, not a number anyone outside this business could independently audit.
